# Vibe and inspiration

Goal: understand what the user wants their product to look and feel like, in words both of you agree on, before any design or front-end work. Save the result in project memory (`docs/vibe.md`, see `references/workspace.md`).

---

## 1. Ask for inspiration

Ask for **three to ten images** of products, websites, apps, or anything else whose look they like. Explain why in one line: "Pictures say more than adjectives; I'll look for what they have in common."

| Source | How to share | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Pinterest (https://www.pinterest.com) | Save images, then upload them here, or take screenshots | Links often need a login, so uploads are more reliable |
| Dribbble (https://dribbble.com) | Screenshot shots you like and upload them | Good for app and website UI |
| Figma (https://www.figma.com) | Share a frame link if a Figma connection is available; otherwise export or screenshot | With a connection you can read the frames directly |
| Anything else | Screenshots of apps, websites, packaging, interiors, posters | Non-digital inspiration is welcome |

Also ask: "Is there anything you dislike? One or two examples help as much as likes." Save images in the project folder (`docs/inspiration/`) when one exists.

Respect copyright: inspiration informs the direction. Never copy someone else's design, logo, or images into the product.

---

## 2. Find at least three patterns

Look across all images and name **at least three patterns**, each with evidence. Check these dimensions:

| Dimension | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Color | Light or dark, warm or cool, muted or saturated, how many accent colors |
| Typography | Serif or sans-serif, large bold headings or quiet small text |
| Layout and density | Lots of whitespace or packed with content; grid, cards, or long scrolling pages |
| Shapes | Sharp corners or rounded; lines, borders, shadows |
| Imagery | Photography or illustration; people or objects; bright studio or natural light |
| Motion and feel | Calm or playful, premium or friendly, technical or human |

Format:

```
Patterns I see (from your 6 images)
1. Calm, light backgrounds with lots of whitespace (5 of 6 images)
2. Large serif headings with small, quiet body text (4 of 6)
3. Earthy accent colors: terracotta, olive, sand (all 6)
One that differs: image 4 is dark and neon. Is that a direction or just a favorite?
```

Then ask the user to confirm, correct, or drop each pattern. Only confirmed patterns go into memory as decisions; the rest are notes.

---

## 3. Clarify what is vague

If a pattern or wish is vague ("modern", "clean", "fun"), ask one question with options and examples:

"When you say 'modern', which is closer?
- A) Minimal: white space, few colors, simple shapes (think a calm banking app)
- B) Bold: big type, strong colors, playful shapes (think a music festival site)
- C) Soft: rounded corners, pastels, friendly illustrations (think a children's learning app)"

---

## 4. Explain design terms

Whenever you use a design or image term, **name it, then explain it in one line with an example**. Use this list as a starting point:

| Term | Plain explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Whitespace | Empty space around things, which makes a page feel calm | A product photo with lots of space around it |
| Color palette | The small set of colors a product uses everywhere | Cream background, black text, one orange accent |
| Accent color | The one color that draws attention, usually on buttons | A green "Buy" button on a grey page |
| Contrast | How strongly things differ, such as dark text on a light background | Easy to read vs. grey on grey |
| Serif / sans-serif | Fonts with small "feet" on letters (serif) or without (sans-serif) | Newspaper headlines vs. most app text |
| Typography hierarchy | Size and weight differences that show what to read first | A big title, a medium subtitle, small body text |
| Hero section | The large first block at the top of a website | A full-width photo with one sentence and a button |
| Grid | Invisible columns that keep things aligned | Product cards in neat rows of three |
| Card | A box grouping related content | A recipe tile with photo, title, and time |
| Minimalism | Only what is necessary, nothing decorative | Few colors, simple icons, lots of whitespace |
| Flat design | No shadows or 3D effects; solid colors | Simple icons on plain colors |
| Glassmorphism | Frosted, see-through panels over a background | A blurred menu bar over a photo |
| Brutalism | Raw, bold, intentionally unpolished look | Huge black text, harsh borders, no rounded corners |
| Bento grid | Content arranged in tiles of different sizes, like a lunch box | A homepage with one big tile and several small ones |
| Mood board | A collage of images that sets the direction | The inspiration images you share with me |
| Dark mode | A version with dark backgrounds and light text | Most phone apps at night |
| Micro-interaction | A small animation that responds to an action | A heart that pops when you tap "like" |
| Flat lay | A photo taken straight from above of objects on a surface | Products arranged on a table, shot from above |
| Lifestyle photography | Products shown in real use | A mug in someone's hands at breakfast |
| Aspect ratio | The width-to-height shape of an image | Square (1:1) vs. widescreen (16:9) |

---

## 5. Turn patterns into direction

After confirmation, write a short design direction in memory:
- Colors: background, text, one or two accents (with example values if the user agrees)
- Type: heading style and body style
- Shapes: corner style, borders or shadows
- Imagery: photography or illustration, and its style
- Feel in three words (for example "calm, warm, honest")
- Do not: what the user disliked

Use this direction for every design and front-end task, and link it from relevant tickets. If the user later shares new inspiration, repeat the analysis and say what changed.
